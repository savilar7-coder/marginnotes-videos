In it he wrote down the governor in the square. He did not call it a crime. He called it "worthy of notice, and to be imitated by others."

He addressed the book to one of the Medici. The same family whose jailers had hung him from that rope. He wanted his desk back.

Other men, he writes, bring a prince horses, weapons, cloth of gold. He brings what fourteen years of watching taught him. Not the world as it ought to be. "The real truth of the matter."

There is no evidence the Medici ever read it.

He waited the rest of his life. When the Medici were thrown out again, he hurried home, hoping. He was fifty-eight. The desk went to another man.

Eleven days later he was dead. He never saw the book printed. He never once heard his name used as a word.
