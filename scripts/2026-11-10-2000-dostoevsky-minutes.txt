[briskly] You'd stop wasting days if you knew how few were left. Right?

In 1849, Dostoevsky was minutes from a firing squad. Then, a pardon.

In The Idiot, he gave that morning to a man who swore:

[slowly] "How I should grudge and count up every minute of it, so as to waste not a single instant!"

Afterwards? He wasted many, and many a minute.

[quietly] Knowing doesn't save the minutes. Starting again does.
