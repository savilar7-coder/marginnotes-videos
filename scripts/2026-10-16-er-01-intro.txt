Welcome to the Evening Review.

This is a practice that is about two thousand years old. The Roman philosopher Seneca learned it from a teacher named Sextius, and he did it every night. When the lamp was taken away, and the house went quiet, he went back over his whole day. Not to punish himself. To see it clearly.

Tonight, you'll do the same. Three questions, the same ones Seneca asked. I'll ask each one, and then I'll give you quiet time to think, or to write in your journal.

There are no wrong answers. One honest line is enough.
