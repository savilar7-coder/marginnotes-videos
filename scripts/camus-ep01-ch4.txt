That's his question. And it doesn't only come after a war.

For most of us the rock is fine, for a while. You do the routine and it works. Until one morning it doesn't. You stop at the kettle, or the door, or the platform, and a small question slips in.

"But one day the 'why' arises."

Why am I doing this? We're built to need a reason; we want our days to add up to something. And when we ask the world what it's all for, the world doesn't answer.

Camus calls that collision the absurd. In plain words: you asking "why?", and the world saying nothing back.
