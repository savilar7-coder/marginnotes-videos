He has spent eight months imprisoned. Among the accusations against him is reading a forbidden letter aloud at Petrashevsky's gatherings. The letter attacks the religious and political order. Now an official reads his death sentence.

White execution shirts. A cross to kiss. The first three prisoners are tied to posts.

Dostoevsky embraces the men beside him. In the letter he writes afterward, he remembers thinking of his brother, Mikhail, and how much he loves him.

Then the signal to stand down.

The men at the posts are brought back. Another announcement: the Emperor has granted them their lives.

The decision has already been made. The official instructions require the preparations for execution before the prisoners hear that they will live.

Dostoevsky's actual sentence is four years of hard labour, followed by service as a private soldier.

He leaves the square alive. That same day, back in the fortress, he writes to Mikhail.
