Camus finishes the book that February. For one moment, the work is done.

The next year the tuberculosis comes back, in his other lung. Camus is sent to the mountains of France to rest, a long way from the sea, and the war shuts the way home behind him. He keeps writing.

And Sisyphus? He has reached the foot of the hill. Camus leaves him there, facing the stone, his back to us, and does not set him free. All the way down, we never got close enough to see his face. The gods were sure what was on it. Camus ends by telling us what to picture there.

One must imagine Sisyphus happy.
