In February 1941, a young man is finishing an argument with the gods. He is in Oran, on the coast of Algeria, bent over a page. His name is Albert Camus. He is twenty-seven, his lungs are bad, and the world is at war.

The argument is a book, and its last pages are set on this hill. The book asks one question: if the world gives us no reason for being here, is life worth living at all? For his answer, Camus goes to the one man who has every reason to say no. And the case has to be made before Sisyphus reaches the bottom.
