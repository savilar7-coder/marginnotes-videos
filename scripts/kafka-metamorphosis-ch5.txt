He is still Gregor, and it is still his room. But the family takes in three lodgers, and nobody tells them what lives next door. They want order, so whatever is in their way ends up in his room: the ash bin, the kitchen rubbish.

One evening his door is left ajar. The lodgers eat at the family's table, and in the dark Gregor listens to their teeth. I am hungry, he thinks, but not for that.

Then Greta plays her violin for them. They are bored. Gregor crawls out toward her, dragging dust and threads. This, he feels, is the food he has been hungry for. Can he be an animal, if music moves him like this?

The lodgers see him, and give notice.

Greta brings her hand down on the table. We must try to get rid of it, she says. It. She will not say her brother's name in front of this monster. How can it be Gregor? If it were Gregor, he would have left long ago, of his own accord.
