And soon he finds out where his soul has been.

"My soul leads me into the desert, into the desert of my own self."

Whatever he pictured, it wasn't sand. Hot, empty, nothing to drink. And he works out why. For years, everything he had went outward. Into patients, books, his reputation, the next thing. Nothing went in. So nothing grew in.

Here's our reading, and it runs through this whole series. Feeling empty after you get what you wanted doesn't have to mean you picked the wrong life. It can mean you've only ever watered the outside of it.

He also learns what solitude is. Being alone in a room isn't it. You can sit alone and still be crowded by your plans, your arguments, your phone. Real solitude, he says, starts when you reach the desert inside, and nobody's there either. Not even the version of you that performs.

That's why we avoid it. Being alone was never the hard part. Being alone with nothing to do shows you how little you've planted.

Most people who get this far think the desert is the bad part. It isn't. The hard part is what he has to do next. Nothing.
