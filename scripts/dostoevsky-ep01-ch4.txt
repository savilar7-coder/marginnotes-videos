Nicholas the First had already decided. His instructions sent the prisoners through the preparations for death before anyone told them that they would live.

Dostoevsky hears his new sentence: four years of hard labour in Siberia, followed by service as a private soldier.

He leaves the square alive and returns to the fortress in custody. That same day, back in his cell, he writes to his brother. The letter begins with what happened that morning: the sentence, the shirts, the cross, the three men at the posts. He names the friends he embraced.

He tells Mikhail that, in those last minutes, he thought of him and his family. Now he can address the letter to him. He asks his brother not to grieve. He asks him to look after his health. There are people he wants Mikhail to thank, friends he wants him to greet on his behalf.
