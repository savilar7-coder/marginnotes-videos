His city was Florence, in Italy. For sixty years one rich family had run it: the Medici. Then the city threw them out and governed itself. He worked for that new government.

It sent him to stand in the courts of kings and popes, and report what he saw. He learned to watch the hands, not the faces.

And he loved it. He once told a friend that he could not talk about silk, or wool, or profit and loss. He could only talk about the state. About power.

One winter the city sent him to the court of Cesare Borgia: young, the Pope's son, and conquering fast.

Borgia had a newly taken province to tame, so he gave it to a governor. The governor was fast, and he was cruel. Soon the province was quiet. And full of hatred.

On the morning after Christmas, the town woke to find the governor in the public square, cut in two.

Machiavelli was there. That night he reported to Florence that the cause of death "is not precisely known." Only that Borgia had shown "he has the power to make and unmake men at his will."

He watched the crowd. Years later he could still describe them: "at once satisfied and dismayed."

Borgia, he decided, had let his man do the cruel work, and then killed him to win the people.
