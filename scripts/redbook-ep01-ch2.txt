Autumn, 1913. Jung is travelling alone, wide awake. And he sees a flood cover Europe, from the North Sea to the Alps. Towns under water. Thousands of drowned bodies. Then the whole sea turns to blood.

Two weeks later he sees it again, worse. And he wonders, seriously, whether he is losing his mind.

Now, here's why that scared him. He wasn't a lost man. He was thirty-eight, one of the best-known psychiatrists in Europe, with a wife, four children and a fifth on the way, a big house on Lake Zurich and a waiting room full of patients. Until that January he was also Freud's chosen heir. Then Freud ended their friendship, and most of their circle went with Freud.

Notice what broke first. Not the career. The map. Jung had a man who told him who he was. Now he didn't.

Later, in the Red Book, he describes where he stood. He had won honor, power, wealth, knowledge, every kind of happiness he had wanted. And then the wanting stopped, and something like horror moved in where it used to be.

That's the car outside the house. Same minute, different century.
