His sister Greta, seventeen, goes in with scraps, to learn what he eats. He hides under the sofa, and takes four hours to drag a sheet over it, so she need not see him.

Weeks pass. Gregor learns to climb: the walls, the ceiling. Greta decides he needs space. She and the mother start carrying his furniture out. The mother stops, almost whispering: won't it look as if we have given up on him? Leave it as it was, for when he comes back to us.

Greta insists. Out goes the desk where he did his homework as a boy. Gregor bolts out, climbs the bare wall to the one picture left, a lady in furs, and presses his hot belly to the glass. This, at least, nobody is taking.

The mother comes back and sees a huge brown stain on the flowered wallpaper. She cries out before she knows it is Gregor, and falls across the sofa.
