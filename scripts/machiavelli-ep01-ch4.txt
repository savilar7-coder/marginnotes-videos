He did more than watch. He raised Florence an army of its own: farm boys with pikes. Then, in 1512, a Spanish army brought the Medici back. His farm boys were sent to stop it. They ran.

So the government he served fell. After fourteen years he was dismissed, and barred from the building he had worked in.

Three months later a plot against the Medici was uncovered. His name was on a list one of the plotters had dropped. He was arrested.

They used the rope. Wrists tied behind the back. Hauled to the ceiling. Dropped. By his own count, six times.

We know his count because he put it in a poem, from his cell, addressed to the Medici lord of the city. "I have, Giuliano, a pair of shackles on my legs, and six drops of the rope on my shoulders." He made it a joke.

Weeks later a Medici was elected Pope. Florence celebrated, the prisons were opened, and he walked out.

He was forty-three. What he had left was a small farm, seven miles outside the city. On a clear day he could see its cathedral dome.
