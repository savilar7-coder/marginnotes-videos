Friday night, you finally clear the inbox. Monday morning, it's full again.

Train. Desk. Lunch. Desk. Train. Sleep.

You push the same rock up the same hill all week.

Albert Camus published almost that exact list in 1942. A young writer from Algiers, in a book called The Myth of Sisyphus. He called it the same rhythm, Monday to Saturday.

When he finished it, he'd been sick for ten years. France, which ruled Algeria then, had just fallen to Germany. And he'd just lost his job.

And he ended that book by saying that a man condemned to push a rock up a hill forever, for nothing, could be happy.

So how does a sick, jobless man in a defeated country end up there? And if he could, could you?

Tonight: the question that hit him, and the one moment in the myth that interested him most. It isn't the push.
