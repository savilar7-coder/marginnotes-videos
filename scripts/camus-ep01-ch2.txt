Camus's father was killed in the First World War before Albert was one year old. The boy grew up in a cramped flat in Belcourt, a poor district of Algiers. His mother cleaned other people's houses. She was partly deaf and couldn't read.

But he was bright. His grandmother wanted him out earning money as soon as possible. A schoolteacher named Louis Germain pushed back, and got him a scholarship. For the first time there was a door out: school, university, philosophy, maybe a life teaching it.

Then, at seventeen, tuberculosis, in those days often a death sentence. He survived, but it never fully left him. And years later, because of it, he wasn't allowed to sit the exam he needed for a career teaching philosophy.

He'd climbed for years toward that door, and at the top it was shut.
