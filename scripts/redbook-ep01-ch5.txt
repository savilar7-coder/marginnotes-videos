Jung doesn't fix the desert. He can't. He tries what a clever man tries. He thinks about it. He explains it. He gets impatient with it. None of that makes rain.

The next part of the book is about waiting, and he hates it. He has spent his whole life being a man who acts, and now the only instruction is: stay. And the voice in the desert is blunt about it.

"Can you not wait? Should everything fall into your lap, ripe and finished?"

Earlier he'd admitted that no amount of cleverness makes a garden out of a soul. So he does the only thing left. He goes back to the sand, night after night, and stays.

We think that's the whole instruction: stay long enough for something to grow.

We treat waiting as dead time between the things that count. In this book, waiting is the only work the desert accepts.

And notice where it happened. He didn't leave his family or move to a monastery. He went back to the same desk, in the same house, every night. His desert was a few steps from the dinner table.

So why does a hundred-year-old notebook matter at your car door? Because the spirit of this time got louder. Jung had newspapers and a coming war. You have a feed that never ends. Every empty minute gets filled before it can turn into a desert. Which means nothing gets the chance to grow there either.

Two honest warnings. Jung could afford his desert: money, a big house, his evenings. Most people's desert is ten minutes at six in the morning. That's fine. The desert is measured in honesty, not in hours.

And what Jung did next was dangerous, and he said so. These essays retell what happened to him. They're not a prescription. If the emptiness looks like depression, if you're not sleeping, not eating, not caring, that's a doctor's door, not a desert.

But if it's only the car-door minute, here's the smallest version of what he did. Tonight, ten minutes, no phone in the room. Ask his question, not yours. Not "what should I do?" Ask "where did I go?" Write whatever comes, even if it's nothing. Don't fix it. Come back tomorrow.

You won't find a garden on the first night. Jung didn't. The first night is for finding out it's sand, and staying anyway.
