A letter has come from Vienna. It says you may be ill, and cannot see it.

January, 1913. You are Carl Jung, thirty-seven, and the letter is from Sigmund Freud, the man who named you his heir. He is ending the friendship, entirely.

You answer in three days. You agree, and you write: the rest is silence.

After that, you are suspended in mid-air.

By autumn you will think you are going mad. Past eighty, you will call these the most important years of your life.

Twice you go back over your whole life, looking for what is wrong with you. You find nothing.

By day you hold on. You have patients, a wife, four children, a fifth on the way. They are your proof that you are an ordinary man, and that you actually exist.
