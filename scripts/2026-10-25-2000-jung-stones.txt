[briskly] Lost at thirty-seven, he went back to playing with stones.

He had been Freud's chosen heir. Then they split, and he lost his footing.

Then he remembered something. As a boy, he loved building little houses.

[slowly] "There is still life in these things."

[faster] So he gathered stones from the lake shore. Cottages. A castle. A whole village.

He felt ashamed. [pause] He did it anyway, after lunch, whenever the weather allowed.

He called it a turning point. That game freed the fantasies that became his Red Book.
