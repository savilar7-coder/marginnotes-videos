Everybody watches the rock. Camus watches the man. Sisyphus turns round, and now comes the only part of the day when his hands are empty. He walks down at a heavy, even pace.

Going up, there is only the stone. Coming down, there is time to think. From here Sisyphus can see all of it: the rock waiting at the foot of the hill, and the track back up.

If he could hope, if at every step he thought, this time it stays, where would the punishment be? But the gods left him no hope. There is no last day. The rock is only the tool. The knowing is the punishment.

Some days, Camus admits, Sisyphus goes down in grief, remembering the sea. Those days belong to the rock.
