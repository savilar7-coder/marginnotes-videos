[briskly] Kafka wrote his father a letter forty-five pages long. His father never read a word.

It opens: [slowly] "You asked me recently why I claim to be afraid of you."

And deep inside: [slowly] "My writing was all about you."

But it never arrived. He gave it to his mother to deliver. She never did.

[quietly] Some letters are not meant to arrive. Writing them is the point.
