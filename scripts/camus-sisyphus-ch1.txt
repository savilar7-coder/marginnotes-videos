On a bare hill, a man gets a huge rock to the top. It rolls straight back down. It always will.

He turns to follow it.

Look at his face.

You can't. He is too far away. And the oldest poem about this hill is no help. Homer gives us two hands on the stone, sweat, dust rising from his head. No face.

The gods think they know what is on that face. The man is Sisyphus, and this hill is their punishment for him: work with no use and no hope. They reckoned nothing could be worse.
