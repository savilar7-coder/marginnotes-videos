One day you'll realize most of the things you were afraid of never happened.

The call you dreaded. The talk you rehearsed all night. The disaster you were sure was coming.

You lived through all of it. In your head.

Seneca noticed this two thousand years ago. He wrote to a friend:

"We suffer more often in imagination than in reality."

And then he named the three tricks your mind plays on you:

"some things torment us more than they ought; some torment us before they ought; and some torment us when they ought not to torment us at all."

More than they should. Before they should. When they shouldn't at all.

So tonight, when your mind starts the movie again, ask which trick this is.

Because the thing you fear is not here. Only the fear is.

Send this to someone who overthinks everything.
