You've already lived through things that never happened.

Seneca wrote to a friend about exactly this, two thousand years ago.

"There are more things, Lucilius, likely to frighten us than there are to crush us; we suffer more often in imagination than in reality."

He sorted the worry three ways. Some things torment us more than they ought. Some, before they ought. And some, when they ought not to torment us at all.

His test, for the three a.m. thoughts: "consider whether your proofs of future trouble are sure."

Tonight, pick the worry that kept you up. Ask it: what's my proof?

No proof? It hasn't happened. Don't live it twice.

Save this for tonight.
