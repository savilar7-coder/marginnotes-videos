In 1913, Carl Jung, a famous Swiss doctor of the mind, had that same minute, and he spent years writing down what came next, by hand, in a red leather book.

He had everything he'd ever wanted. So why did he start to think he was going mad?

He showed that book to almost no one. After he died, his family kept it from the public for nearly fifty years. We call it the Red Book.

And his answer to the emptiness wasn't a plan, or a pill, or a new life. It was a place. A desert.

This is the first of ten essays on that book. Tonight: why the emptiness came just as everything went right, the strange question he asked, and the one thing he says you have to do in the desert. Almost nobody wants to.
