Sisyphus, as Camus tells it, has already died once. But he talked his way out of the underworld, for one short visit to the daylight. And up there he saw the sea again. He felt the sun, the warm stones. So Sisyphus stayed. He stayed for years, until a god took him by the collar and dragged him away, to where the rock was waiting.

Camus knows that sea. He grew up beside it.

On the page, Camus follows Sisyphus up. One cheek pressed to the stone. A shoulder braced under the weight. A foot wedged. Two hands caked with earth.

Then the top. For one moment, the work is done.

The rock tips, and goes.
