A ruler, he writes, must choose: to be loved, or to be feared. "It is much safer to be feared than loved."

Because men, he says, are "ungrateful, fickle, false, cowardly." While you are winning, they are yours. When you need them, they turn.

And a ruler who means to last must "know how to do wrong."

The man who wrote that was a civil servant. Fourteen years at a desk.
